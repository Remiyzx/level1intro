Unlike Western/English names (First name + Last name), Chinese names always place the Family Name (Surname) FIRST, followed by the Given Name.

💡 Fun Trivia: Did You Know?

1. What Does "Chinese" Actually Mean? (Language vs. Ethnicity vs. Nationality)

In English, the word "Chinese" carries 3 distinct meanings that often confuse learners:
• Ethnicity / Heritage (华裔 / Orang Cina): Refers to Chinese cultural descent regardless of citizenship (e.g., Malaysian Chinese / Wǎnyì Huárén).
• Nationality (中国公民): Refers to a citizen of China (China Chinese).
• Language (中文 / 汉语): Refers to the spoken and written language family.

2. Hànyǔ vs. Zhōngwén vs. Huáyǔ vs. Pǔtōnghuà (汉语 / 中文 / 华语 / 普通话)

Ever wondered why there are so many terms for "Chinese language"? They all refer to standard Mandarin, but carry different regional nuances:
• Hànyǔ (汉语): "Language of the Han people" — the formal linguistic name used in textbooks (like our course title!).
• Zhōngwén (中文): "Chinese Language & Script" — the standard general term understood globally.
• Huáyǔ (华语): "Ethnic Chinese Language" — the official & widely used term in Malaysia & Singapore!
• Pǔtōnghuà (普通话) / Guóyǔ (国语): "Common Speech" (Mainland China official term) / "National Language" (Taiwan official term).

3. Chinese Has NO Conjugations or Tenses!

Unlike English or French (eat / ate / eaten / eating), Chinese verbs NEVER change form! You just add time words like "yesterday" or "tomorrow".
E.g., "Yesterday I eat rice" → Super logical & simple!

🌱 Language Learning Mindset: Step-by-Step (循序渐进)

Learning a new language is a step-by-step process. To build a strong and accurate foundation, we will NOT jump straight into phrase memorization like *"Good morning"* (Zǎo'ān), *"Thank you"* (Xièxie), *"Goodbye"* (Zàijiàn), or *"Ice cream"* (Bīngqílín).

Instead, we start with the core fundamentals today: mastering Chinese Pinyin sounds and tones starting from the basic simple vowels: a, o, e, i, u, ü!
